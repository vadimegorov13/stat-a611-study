4.3 III. Quartiles
The second quartile is the median of the entire ordered dataset:
𝑄2 = e𝑥 .
The first (lower) quartile 𝑄1 is the median of the lower half, and the third (upper) quartile 𝑄3 is the median of
the upper half.
Even and Odd Sample Sizes
If 𝑛 is even, the lower half consists of the smallest 50% of the values and the upper half consists of the largest
50%.
If 𝑛 is odd, 𝑄2 is the middle value. For the convention used in class, the median is included in both halves:
the lower half ends with 𝑄2, and the upper half begins with 𝑄2.

4.5 V. Outliers
A value is an outlier if it is at least 1.5(IQR) away from the nearest quartile. If it is at least 3(IQR) away
from the nearest quartile, it is an extreme outlier; otherwise it is a mild outlier.
The four reference points are
𝑄1 − 3IQR, 𝑄 1 − 1.5IQR, 𝑄 3 + 1.5IQR, 𝑄 3 + 3IQR.
𝑄1 − 3IQR 𝑄1 − 1.5IQR 𝑄3 + 1.5IQR 𝑄3 + 3IQR
EXTREME MILD not an outlier MILD EXTREME
In a boxplot, a mild outlier is indicated by a circle ◦, while an extreme outlier is indicated by an asterisk ∗.
Most datasets do not have outliers.
4.6 VI. Boxplots
A boxplot is also called a box-and-whiskers plot. To construct a boxplot:
1. Order the dataset.

3. Determine whether there are outliers.
4. Find

5. Draw the boxplot.

The Five-Number Summary
The five-number summary is
{smallest value, 𝑄 1, 𝑄 2, 𝑄 3, largest value} .
The smallest and largest values in the five-number summary are the actual dataset extremes, even when one of
them is plotted separately as an outlier in the boxplot.
